What this station is for

This station covers the stretch from sound to finished record: recording, mixing, synthesis and sound design, arranging, and how music works inside film and games.

Which stages are covered

  • Recording — microphone placement, the effect of the room, and the trade-offs between close and distant capture.
  • Mixing — dynamics, tone, space and depth: how dozens of tracks become one clear picture.
  • Synthesis and sound design — subtractive, additive, sampling and physical modelling, each with its own character and typical use.
  • MIDI and arranging — describing performance as data; velocity, timing and the deliberate imperfection that makes it breathe.
  • Notation and layout — turning a performance into a score someone else can read.
  • Music for media — how music works in film and games: themes, motifs, loops and cues.

What each entry looks like

  • It starts with the principle: what property of the sound this step is actually changing.
  • Then how to hear it: what differs before and after on the same piece of material.
  • Where an example is possible, it gives a paired comparison rather than describing settings on their own.

How to read it

1. In workflow order — record, mix, arrange, finish is the easiest path through. 2. With one piece of music in hand — comparing against something familiar works far better. 3. Do not mistake settings for conclusions — settings are means; what you hear is the result.

Where it starts

Both halves — production and digital audio — are being added over time. Recording and mixing come first, because the ear built here is what the later discussion of synthesis and arranging depends on. :::
