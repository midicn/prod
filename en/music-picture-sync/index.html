Music and Picture

Sync points, counterpoint and contrast decide whether music follows the image or pushes against it.

1 min readMedia ScoringUpdated 2026-10-28

sync pointcounterpointcontrasteditingscoring

Related

CueProgram MusicRomantic

Picture and music each have a timeline. Music and picture is how they lock together: which beat lands on a door, a turn or an explosion, and which beats are left out of phase.

The problem

Laying music over an image is easy. Deciding where it must align and where it must give way is not. Align everything and the score behaves like sound effects; align nothing and the two feel pasted together. Sync points are that contract.

Music and picture

Hard sync puts an accent on the same frame as an action; soft sync aligns only the mood. Counterpoint lets the music state what the image does not, such as a character smiling over a descending bass. Contrast sets a cheerful dance against violence. The three can alternate in a scene.

Practice and judgement

Count the beats: footsteps, doors, cuts. Mark the three to five points that matter and leave the rest to the phrase. Listen without the image, then watch without the music. If both stand alone, they will not drag each other down.

What to listen for

Hear Flight of the Bumblebee create chase, the Swan Lake theme breathe with the scene, and the accents in the game chase loop. The demo is a fast flute run; cuts usually land on its accents.

Common misunderstandings

Sync does not mean a sound for every action. Music does not always follow the mood either; contrast is deliberate. When a temp track is replaced, sync points survive but the phrasing no longer fits, so the cue needs rewriting.
