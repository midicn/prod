Hook Design

A hook must be short, repeatable and transformable, and needs three appearances to stick.

1 min read25 characters条目ArrangingUpdated 2026-10-28

Related

A hook must be short, repeatable and transformable. It is usually a rhythm, an interval or a single melodic phrase, and it needs at least three appearances to stick. The arranging job is to put it where it is clearest, then use timbre and harmony to make each return feel new.

The problem

Too much material leaves listeners unsure which phrase matters. The hook sits under a dense accompaniment, or repeats unchanged until it loses its pull.

Texture or frequency band

Hooks usually occupy the upper midrange and should sit above the accompaniment. Bass and harmony can yield, and drums can simplify. The first appearance needs space, the second can add harmony, and the third can change timbre or key.

Approach

Write a phrase of four to eight beats and check that it can be hummed alone. Extract its rhythmic skeleton and try transposing, augmenting or turning it minor. On each return, change only one parameter, whether timbre, register or harmony, while keeping the rhythmic outline.

What to listen for

Hear the stepwise contour of the Ode to Joy theme. Hear the short phrase repeat in the harmonica version of The Sound of Silence. Then hear the figure in the first two bars of a game battle loop and notice how the loop makes it memorable.

Common misunderstandings

A hook is not the same as a chorus, since it can live in an intro or a bass line. Repetition is not literal copying either, because each return should change a little.
